1 · Arrive

You are not quite the same person in every room

A place can say, out loud and often, that it values something — and be, underneath that language, organized to produce something else entirely. You inherited that gap the moment you walked in, the same way you inherited the room's temperature.

2 · Two Real Institutions

Nursing homes and correctional facilities, sharing nothing but ownership

Case One: Independent published research found facilities acquired by private equity firms provide 33 fewer minutes of direct daily care per resident than comparable non-profit facilities, and linked PE ownership to a measurable increase in short-term patient mortality.

Case Two: A private, for-profit correctional facility shows staff turnover near 43 percent, a share of revenue allocated to actual rehabilitation programming under 1 percent, and a measurable increase in recidivism compared to publicly operated facilities.

The population, mission, and regulatory system are all completely different. What's identical, and why does that consistency matter more than either case alone?

3 · The Smoothness Caution

"It's working fine" is not evidence

An institution can run without visible friction — meeting its numbers, passing its audits, keeping its doors open — while the actual thing it was built to produce quietly stops happening at all. Absence of visible friction is not evidence of health.

Why are the people closest to the actual cost often the people with the least power to make anyone look?

5 · BioBond, Generalized

Four scales, one definition

A BioBond is any relational condition, at any scale, that sustains or enhances an agent's capacity for repair — a trusted friend (individual/relational), mutual trust grown through shared work (peer/community), a sustained external network like Oliver Tambo's (institutional/political), or a designed change in an institution's relationship to its own funders (financial/structural).

A friend is easy to recognize as sustaining you. Why does a restructured relationship between a nursing home chain and its banks operate by the identical logic, even though it feels like nothing at all?

6 · Building Alongside

Unchained Industries

Founded by someone who had himself been incarcerated, Unchained Industries operates as a networked structure in the correctional sector, following the identical strategy Maathai used: not reforming the extractive structure from within, but building a working alternative alongside it until its results become too documented to ignore.

He wasn't an outside reformer proposing what incarcerated people probably need. Why does having actually lived inside the extractive structure matter for what he was able to build?
