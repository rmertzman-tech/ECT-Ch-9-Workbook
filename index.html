1 · ENTER — Institutional Doing

The Student Success Center

A college creates a Student Success Center. Its stated purpose is to help students solve problems before they derail progress. After a year, the center reports that average wait time fell from 22 minutes to 8 minutes and that more cases are being closed each day. Administrators praise the center as a success.
Doing lens: An institution is not only what it says. It is also the repeated pattern of attention, incentives, decisions, and consequences it makes easier to reproduce.

3 · REVEAL — What Gets Rewarded?

The mission is not the metric

Advisors are evaluated partly on cases closed per day and average appointment length . Complex cases involving financial aid, disability accommodations, housing instability, or immigration paperwork usually take much longer than simple scheduling questions.
Mapper
Trace the route the reward system makes easier.
Challenger
Find a reasonable alternative explanation.
License-checker
Ask what the evidence does and does not yet justify.
Board prompt: Stated purpose / Actual reward / Repeated route. Do not decide yet whether anyone is acting badly.
Instructor reveal
4 · SMOOTHNESS — What Disappears?

The dashboard keeps improving

Wait time continues to fall. But some students with complex cases are referred repeatedly, retell the same story to different staff members, miss financial-aid or registration deadlines, and stop returning. Those outcomes are not included in the center's main performance dashboard.
Smoothness Caution: absence of visible friction is not evidence of institutional health. A process can become smoother partly because difficult cases, hidden costs, or affected people fall outside the metric.
Chapter pressure test
5 · PATTERN — The Same Signature?

Use the chapter's two institutional examples carefully

The chapter presents two very different institutions—PE-acquired nursing homes and a private correctional facility—and asks whether a similar structural signature appears when financial or organizational rewards pull away from the institution's stated care or rehabilitation purpose.

Question What to look for
Mission What is the institution publicly for?
Operative reward What behavior is actually rewarded, protected, or made easy to repeat?
Hidden cost Who or what carries costs that the success metric may not count?
Repair capacity Can people inside the system detect, report, and correct the problem?
EHI firewall: recurring structural similarity can justify a stronger question or hypothesis. It does not by itself prove a single cause.

6 · REPAIR — BioBond Generalized

What sustains the capacity to detect and repair?

BioBond: any relational condition, at any scale, that sustains or enhances an agent's capacity for repair.

In the Student Success Center, a repair-supporting relation might be a stable advisor relationship, a peer navigator, an escalation route, protected time for complex cases, or a funding relationship that does not punish slower high-complexity work.

Design alternative practice
7 · BUILD ALONGSIDE

Do not only criticize the old route—prototype another one

A small advising team is allowed to pilot a Complex Case Lane . Advisors in that lane are not evaluated by appointment length. Students keep one primary advisor, and the team tracks whether cases are actually resolved rather than merely closed.
Licensing + critical thinking

Find the jumps that need bridges

Route:
Stated mission → metric → repeated behavior → observed pattern → structural diagnosis → redesign → authorization
Useful firewalls:
stated purpose ≠ operative reward
metric ≠ mission
smooth operation ≠ health
recurring pattern ≠ proven cause
diagnosis ≠ authorization to redesign

10 · TRANSFER — A New Institutional Doing

The Fast Discharge Metric

A hospital unit is praised because average discharge time falls sharply. Staff later report that patients with complicated home-care needs are more likely to call back, return to the emergency department, or rely on family members who were not prepared for the care burden.

11 · CARRY FORWARD

What should survive this chapter?

Freshman-level close: Better diagnosis does not mean “institutions are bad.” It means we learn to compare what a system says, what it repeatedly rewards, what costs disappear from view, and what would let the system notice and repair itself.
